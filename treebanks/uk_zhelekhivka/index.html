Description

UD Ukrainian-Zhelekhivka is a treebank of a historical regional standard of Ukrainian used in the late 19th and early 20th centuries mostly in the territories that were historically part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. The treebank contains approximately 10,000 tokens from three complete newspaper articles.

UD Ukrainian-Zhelekhivka represents a historical regional variety of Ukrainian written in the Zhelekhivka orthography — a phonetic spelling system first set forth by Yevhen Zhelekhivskyi in his Malorusko-nimetskyi slovar (1886) and revised in 1904 by the Shevchenko Scientific Society in Ruska pravopys’ zi slovarcem. The orthography was the dominant standard in Galicia and Bukovina, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, and remained in use into the 1920s–1930s, by which time these territories had become part of Poland, Romania, and Czechoslovakia respectively.

The texts in this treebank date from the late 19th and early 20th centuries and represent both variants of Zhelekhivka — the original Zhelekhivskyi spelling and the 1904 Shevchenko Scientific Society revision. Zhelekhivka differs from the modern Ukrainian orthographic standard in a number of respects. Notably, it differs in word segmentation: the reflexive clitic ся and the future-tense markers му, меш, ме, мемо, мете, муть most often follow the verb as separate words; particles such as ж, же, б, би are usually, though not always consistently, written together with the preceding word. Other characteristic features include the letter ї in place of historical е and ѣ (лїд, слїдство), the absence of an apostrophe after labial consonants before я, ю, є (пять, звязок), a soft sign marking palatalization of с, з, ц before a following soft consonant (сьвіт, сьвято), and the letter ґ in borrowings (ґенеральний, ґазета); for a fuller list of features and examples, see [1].

[1] Yurii Chemerys, Olesia Nakhlik, Andriy Rysin, Maria Shvedova. Normalization of a Historic Western Ukrainian orthographic system Zhelekhivka in the Ukrainian Language Reference Corpus (GRAC). In Proceedings of the IEEE 18th International Conference on Computer Sciences and Information Technologies (CSIT). 20 Oct. 2023. Lviv, Ukraine. DOI: 10.1109/CSIT61576.2023.10324255.
