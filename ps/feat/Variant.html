Variant: alternative form of word

Values: Short Long Weak Dir

The Variant feature helps distinguish alternate word forms for the same lemma and set of features. The Short value of this feature is used in several situations, while the other possible values are used only for particular pronouns.

Short: short variant

This feature is used for special short variant of various verbal, nominal and pronominal forms:

(1) The subjunctive of the verb تلل tlël “to go” is irregular and consist of a word لاړ lâṛ and finite forms of the auxiliary verb شول šwël (perfective counterpart of کېدل kedë́l “to become”). The form لاړ lâṛ is currently annotated as a “short infinitive”, i.e. with VerbForm=Inf and Variant=Short, in contrast to the proper (perfective) infinitive لاړل lâṛë́l.

(2) Several forms of the past stem may be shortened missing the ل ë́l suffix:

  • (a) past indicative (only when the past stem is different from the present stem),
  • (b) non-direct cases of the infinitive,
  • (c) past participle,
  • (d) conditional form.

NOTE: For a few irregular verbs, these “short” forms may be the only acceptable forms. If this confirms, the feature will be removed from them.

(3) Some inanimate nouns, often with the meaning of a measuring unit, ending in ونه úna in plural, have the plural ending usually shortened to ه a after a numeral or another quantifier.

(4) Some nouns may ending in ونو úno or انو ấno in non-direct cases of plural, may have the ending shortedned to و o.

NOTE: The points (3) and (4) may be the same case, but there are few occurrences of the point (4) in the treebank for now to confirm that.

(5) The remote demonstrative pronoun هفسې háġase “such like that/those” may be shortened to هسې háse.

Examples

(1)

  • e.g.: ‏‏‎غواړم هلته لاړ شم ġwâṛë́m hálta lâṛ šëm “I want to go there”

(2)

  • (a) ما څوکۍ واخیسته mâ cawkë́y wấxista instead of ما څوکۍ واخیستله mâ cawkë́y wấxistëla “I bought a chair”
  • (b) ړنګېدل ṛëngedë́l “to collapse / the collapse” -> د ړنګېدلو dë ṛëngedë́lo or shortened د ړنګېدو dë ṛëngedó “of the collapse”
  • (c) intransitive: راتللی یم râtláy yëm instead of راتلی یم râtlë́lay yëm “I have come”
  • (c) transitive: ما لوستلی دی mâ lwastáy dëy instead of ما لوستی دی mâ lwastë́lay dëy “I have read (it)”
  • (d) لیدای شم lidấy šëm instead of لیدلای شم lidë́lây šëm “I can see”

(3)

  • without number: کیلومترونه kilomitrúna “kilometers”
  • with number: دوه کیلومتره dwa kilomítra “two kilometers”

Long: long variant of proximate demonstrative pronoun

There are two series of proximate demonstrative pronouns: based on the short variant دا dâ and the long variant دغه daġa (this/these), and their. Because the long variant is less frequent and, unlike the short one, it lacks the counterpart series in personal pronouns, the short variant is unmarked and the the long variant is marked with the Variant=Long feature.

Examples

  • (a) دغه daġa, دغۀ dáġë, دغې dáġe, دغو dáġo “this/these”
  • (b) همدغه hamdáġa, همدغۀ hamdáġë, همدغې hamdáġe, همدغو hamdáġo “same”
  • (c) دغسې dáġase “such like this/these”
  • (d) همدغسې hamdáġase “same like this/these”

Weak: weak pronoun

Special type of personal pronouns, used as unstressed predicate arguments of transitive verbs or unstressed possessive pronouns. The weak pronoun یې ye is also used in contracted forms (which are separeted in UD annotation) with some prepositions. (See also PronType.)

The weak pronouns are clitics and usually occupy the second position in the sentence.

The predicate arguments which the verb agree with, can be omitted, but the other argument of transitive verb, i.e. the object in non-past tenses and the subject in past tenses, cannot be omitted and the weak pronoun has to be used at least.

Examples

  • non-past transitive object: .پلار یې وینی plâr ye winí. “The father sees him/her/it/them.”
  • past transitive subject: .کتابونه دې ولوستل kitâbúna de wë́lwastël. “You read the books.”
  • possession: ورور مې wror me instead of more usual زما ورور zmâ wror “my brother”
  • with preposition: پرې pre (پر+یې për+ye) “on it” f, ترې tre (تر+یې tër+ye) “up to it”, پرې خبرې نۀ کوم pre xabáre në́ kawëm “I do not speak it”, lit. “on_it talk not I_do” (about a language)

NOTE: The use with prepositions is debatable. It may be not correct to consider these cases multi-word tokens including the weak pronoun یې ye. This annotation may be changed in the future after more research will be done.

Dir: directional pronoun

Special type of personal pronouns, which are used as verb prefixes with the meaning of a direction and also in contracted forms with some postpositions. In both cases the token is considered a multi-word token in the UD annotation, so the directional pronoun is a separate word. (See also PronType.)

Examples

  • as verb prefix: ورکول warkawë́l “to give” (lit. “to do towards somebody)”, رانیول _râniwë́l “to catch” (something moving towards the actor)
  • with preposition: ورنسره warsará “with him/her/it/them”, درباندې darbấnde “upon you”
