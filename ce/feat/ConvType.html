ConvType: type of converb

Chechen has a number of converbs. Depending on the type, they occur in either subordinate clauses or chained coordinate clauses. The values below list the converbs found in the treebank, other forms are attested in the literature.

CVBant: anterior converb

An anterior converb is formed by adding the suffix -na (or -la following -l) to the perfective stem. It can either occur in chained clauses to express sequential events, or in complex temporal-aspectual constructions.

Examples

  • hinc shaa q’an-j-iella, dwa-eqqi-na…
    now 3SG.REFL old-J-get.CVBant away-drive-CVBant
    ‘Now I’ve got old, he abandoned me…’

CVBsim: simultaneous converb

A simultaneous converb is composed of the imperfective stem and the suffix -(u)sh. It is used to form adverbial clauses that describe eventualties simultaneous with those in the main clause. In addition, it is used in complex temporal-aspectual constructions with the progressive or continuous aspect.

Examples

  • cwacca cho b-oqq-ush, xalla hwa=’a=j-aesta-na
    one.by.one hair B-take-CVBsim hard here=CCONJ=J-untie-PRF
    ‘Pulling hair one by one, she united them with difficulty.’

  • v-oeda-sh xilla v-oqqa stag
    V-go-CVBsim be.PRF V-old man
    ‘the old man was walking’

CVBtemp: temporal converb

A temporal converb is composed of the perfective stem and the suffixes -cha or -nach. It typically occurs in subordinate temporal clauses.

Examples

  • cuo e aell-cha…
    3SG.ERG 3SG.DEM.ABS say-CVBtemp
    ‘when she said it…’

CVBafter: converb “after”

Converbs with the suffix -chol occur in combination with the adverb t’aehwa ‘afterward’.

Examples

  • geenajalchol t’ehwa
    far-J-get.CVBafter after
    ‘after she got away’

CVBimm: immediate converb

An immediate converb is formed by adding the suffix i to the imperfective stem. It has an immediate imperative or iterative, generic reading. This converb occurs in chained constructions only.

Examples

  • hwunax ghoi, dika shi ghash j-aa
    forest.LAT go.CVBimm good two stick J-bring.IMP
    ‘Go to the forest, bring two good sticks.’

CVBas: converb “as soon as”

Converbs composed of the perfective stem and the suffix _ -(n)iahw_ occur in adverbial clauses with the reading ‘as soon as’.

Examples

  • xaejniahw ‘as soon as (she) knew’

CVBbefore: converb “before”

Converbs carrying the suffix -jahw occur in adverbial clauses with the reading ‘before’.

Examples

  • duq xan jalljahw
    much time j-pass.CVBbefore
    ‘soon (lit. before much time passed)’

CVBuntil: converb “until”

Converbs with the suffix -alc (on the infinitive stem) occur in adverbial clauses with the reading ‘until’.

Examples

  • caarna naab qettalc ‘until they fell asleep (lit. them sleep strike.until)’
