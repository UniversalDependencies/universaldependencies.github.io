Case: case

Chechen has a rich paradigm of cases assigned on nominal and pronominal classes, including nouns, pronouns, numerals, determiners, and nominalized verbal forms.

Case Singular Plural
Absolutive Ø -ash, -ij, -i
Ergative -(as), -uo -sh-a
Genitive -(a)n -in
Dative -na, -n -ash-na
Instrumental -(a)ca -ash-ca
Locative -(a)hw -ash-ahw, -kahw
Lative -(a)x -iax, -ix
Ablative -gara -ash-kara
Allative -(a)ga, -ie -ash-ka
Comparative -(a)l -ial, -il

Attributive forms, however, distinguish between the absolutive and the oblique cases only (the latter is marked with the suffix -chu).

Abs: absolutive

The absolutive is assigned on the semantic subject of intransitive verbs and on the direct object of transitive verbs.

Examples

  • byjsa j-alla
    night.ABS J-pass.PRF
    ‘The night passed.’

Erg: ergative

The ergative marks the semantic subject of transitive verbs.

Examples

  • zha.Ø lar-d-oora aas
    sheep.ABS watch-D-make.IMPF 1SG.ERG
    ‘I used to watch sheep.’

Dat: dative

The dative marks the semantic subject of psych verbs such as deeza ‘love, need’, xaza ‘hear’, ga ‘see’, etc. It also marks the indirect object of verbs of contact and physical transfer.

Examples

  • Islam-ana Nadir v-ouza.
    Islam-DAT Nadir.ABS V-know
    ‘Islam knows Nadir.’

Gen: genitive

The genitive marks the possessor in the possessive construction.

Examples

  • Islam-an mashien j-u.
    Islam-GEN car.ABS J-be
    ‘Islam has a car.’

Ins: instrumental

The instrumental marks nominal phrases that have instrumental or comitative readings.

Examples

  • Hara doexka san koch-aca d-ooghu.
    DEM belt.ABS 1SG.GEN dress-INS D-come
    ‘This belt goes well with my dress.’

Cmp: comparative

The comparative is used exclusively in the comparative construction, where it forms an equivalent of a than- clause in English.

Examples

  • san k’ant hwan jow-al liaq-ox v-u
    1.SG.GEN boy.ABS 2SG.GEN jow-CMP tall-CMP V-be
    ‘My son is taller than your girl.’

Loc: locative

The locative marks non-core arguments that denote a location.

Examples

  • Madina-na Islam ghien-ahw go.
    Madina-DAT Islam.ABS dream-LOC see
    ‘Islam dreams about Madina.’

Lat: lative

The lative marks a variety of non-core arguments, including source and goal.

Examples

  • Islam hoqu ghaal-ax ciac-v-aella.
    Islam.ABS this town-LAT impressed-V-get.PRF
    ‘Islam was impressed by this town.’

All: allative

The allative marks the causee in causative constructions; it also marks the addressee of verbs of saying.

Examples

  • V-oqqa-chu stag-as Islam-ie kniiga j-esh-ijt-ina
    V-old-OBL man-ERG Islam-ALL book.ABS J-read-CAUS-PRF
    ‘The old man made Islam read the book.’

  • V-oqqa-chu stag-ie xaettana…
    V-old-OBL man-ALL ask-PRF
    ‘(They) asked the old man…’

Abl: ablative

The ablative marks non-core arguments such as source or direction.

Examples

  • Islam shien ghaala-ra dwa-v-axa-na.
    Islam.ABS 3SG.GEN.REFL town-ABL away-V-go-PRF
    ‘Islam left his town.’

Acc: oblique/accusative

The oblique marks all non-absolutive uses of attributive adjectives and nominalized verbal forms. Following the UD convention, the value Acc is used to mark the oblique case.

Examples

  • V-oqqa-chu stag-ie xaettana…
    V-old-OBL man-ALL ask-PRF
    ‘(They) asked the old man…’
