NounClass: noun class

The feature NounClass refers to grammatical agreement marked in Chechen on a subset of verbs, adjectives, and numerals. There are four primary prefixes that appear on the agreement targets: v-, j-, d-, and b-. A pair of singular and plural agreement prefixes define a grammatical gender. Komen et al. (2021: 324-325) describe a system of five Chechen genders:

Gender Singular Plural Notes
1st/2nd person V/J D V for male, J for female; pronouns only
Human V/J B V for male, J for female
Various B B Minority of B gender
Various D D Minority of D gender
Various J J  

Chechen has ergative-absolutive alignment. Predicates agree in noun class with the absolutive argument, commonly the subject of intransitive predicates or the object of transitive predicates.

Vclass: V-class

The V-class marks agreement with constituents denoting singular human male referents. It is marked with the prefix v-.

Examples

  • vedda “(he) ran away”

Jclass: J-class

The J-class marks agreement with constituents denoting singular human female referents, as well as animals and inanimate entities. It is marked with the prefix j-.

Examples

*jedda “(she) ran away”

Dclass: D-class

The D-class marks agreement with constituents denoting animals and inanimate entities. It is marked with the prefix d-. This class is used as the default citation form for all agreeing predicates.

Examples

  • sa dajna “light vanished (it got dark)”

Bclass: B-class

The B-class marks agreement with constituents denoting animals and inanimate objects, as well as plural human referents. It is marked with the prefix b-.

Examples

  • _b<\b>axch_ “when they went”

References

Komen, Erwin R., Zarina Molochieva, and Johanna Nichols, ‘Chechen and Ingush’, in Maria Polinsky (ed.), The Oxford Handbook of Languages of the Caucasus, Oxford Handbooks.
